Thymus Biology

The thymus is a central organ of the immune system where T-cells are formed and mature.

These cells represent the foundation of adaptive immunity and allow the body to recognize infections, maintain immune balance and provide immune protection.

With age the activity of the thymus gradually decreases.

This process is accompanied by a reduction in the formation of new T-cells and a gradual decline in immune competence.

Understanding thymus biology is an important direction in modern immunology and regenerative medicine.
